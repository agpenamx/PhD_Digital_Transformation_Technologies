# JARVIS research addendum: chemosensory systems, alcohol genetics, and history

**Status:** Preliminary educational research design. November 23, 2026. No individual testimony, case data, genetic information, diagnoses or media are included.

## Overview

This proposal explores intersections among human perception, multisensory integration, alcohol metabolism, public policy and historical religious interpretation. It deliberately distinguishes data, mechanism, association, interpretation and unproven causal explanations.

### Taste and multisensory integration

Taste buds transduce dissolved chemicals. Gustatory afferents travel through cranial nerves VII, IX and X to brainstem nuclei and higher-order neural processing, including thalamic and insular cortical pathways. Flavor integrates olfactory, gustatory, textural, thermal and cognitive input; expectations and attention may influence judgments. Altered flavor perception admits multiple possible causes and should not be automatically attributed to any particular mechanism.

### Alcohol and genetics

Ethanol is metabolized in part by ADH and ALDH enzymes; variants in genes including `ADH1B` and `ALDH2` are associated with differences in metabolism and alcohol-use-disorder risk. Risk is polygenic and socially/environmentally mediated, and individual genotype cannot be inferred from ancestry stories. Avoid genetic essentialism, identifiable family-tree disclosures, and deterministic claims.

### Historical policy research

U.S. national Prohibition began January 17, 1920 and was repealed December 5, 1933. This can serve as a historically bounded context for studying alcohol distribution, enforcement, public health and cohort influences. Policy history does not itself demonstrate heritable biological change.

### Prospective research questions

1. In benign, informed-consent studies, how do water composition, smell, expectations and attention affect blinded flavor judgments?
2. How do sensory, sleep and contextual factors influence perceived flavor, with participants free to stop at any time?
3. Which published population-level gene associations with ethanol metabolism replicate across cohorts after accounting for confounding?
4. Can AI maintain accurate citations and separate phenomenological observations from social, religious and neurophysiological interpretations?

### Agent governance

ARCHIVIST: preservation and provenance; SHERLOCK: alternative hypotheses and disconfirmation; NEURO: source-checked mechanisms; GENETICS: population interpretation and privacy; HISTORY: source-backed chronology; THEOLOGY: context without presupposed physical causation; ETHICS: consent, no intoxicant challenges, no unsafe interventions; HUMAN REVIEW: independent accountability.
