# JARVIS / NEUROFAITH-03 | Gustation, genetics and policy: testable questions
**Status:** academic methodology draft; not medical advice, diagnosis, or evidence of supernatural influence. **Updated:** 2026-11-23.

## Scientific premise
Sensory experiences are observations of perception. They do not, by themselves, establish external causal agents. Unusual taste experiences can be investigated with clinical taste/smell assessment and blinded stimulus protocols, while maintaining privacy and protecting the meaning participants attach to their experiences.

## Taste and perception
The gustatory system comprises oral taste receptor cells, cranial nerves VII, IX, X, the solitary nucleus, thalamic relay, insula and frontal operculum, with orbitofrontal integration of taste, olfaction, context, and reward. Distorted or phantom tastes (dysgeusia/phantogeusia) can have ordinary causes such as medicines, infection, olfactory changes, dental/oral issues and other medical factors. A change in the apparent taste of water is not objective evidence that a specific external entity altered neural signaling.

## Alcohol genetics and lineage
ADH1B and ALDH2 encode enzymes relevant to ethanol and acetaldehyde metabolism. Variants may influence alcohol responses or population-level risk, but alcohol use disorder is polygenic and strongly context-dependent. Ancestry alone cannot establish an individual's genotype, mental state or behavior. Avoid biological essentialism and deterministic "lineage" interpretations. Do not collect anyone's DNA without informed consent.

## Historical natural experiment: US Prohibition
The United States introduced nationwide Prohibition on 1920-01-17 through the 18th Amendment and Volstead Act; it ended on 1933-12-05 upon ratification of the 21st Amendment. The period is useful for examining interactions among legislation, illicit markets, social attitudes, public health, policing and substitution patterns. Historical association alone is insufficient to claim a single directional cause for all later drug trends.

## Proposed safeguards and methods
- Separate anonymous first-person testimony from source-verified measurement and from explanatory hypotheses.
- Use a blind, no-harm taste protocol with independently labeled safe samples, only if approved by a qualified human study reviewer; do not deliberately induce intoxication, sleep loss, distress or sensory symptoms.
- Record exposure, medications, nutrition, dental/ENT changes and possible environmental factors only with consent and protected storage.
- Contrast hypotheses using preregistered outcomes; provide uncertainty bounds and explicit disconfirmation paths.
- In sensitive cases, invite independent evaluation by ENT, addiction medicine or neuroscience clinicians; no AI diagnosis.
- Religious terms remain historical or personal interpretations, not biological disease classifications and not research findings.
