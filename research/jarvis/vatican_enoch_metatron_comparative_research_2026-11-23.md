## Historically distinct source corpora
1. Biblical Enoch is named in Genesis 5:21–24. The Vatican-published New American Bible introduction to Jude says Jude refers to the Book of Enoch in 1:14–15: https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0839/_P12I.HTM
2. **1 Enoch** is an ancient Jewish apocalyptic collection, preserved in full in Ethiopic/Ge'ez and part of the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition; it is not part of the Roman Catholic biblical canon. Scholarly background: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/41986/chapter/355425440
3. **3 Enoch** / Sefer Hekhalot belongs to later Jewish mystical literature and includes the tradition associating Enoch with the figure Metatron. It is **not** the same book as 1 Enoch. https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/3-enoch ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/seal-of-resemblance-full-of-wisdom-and-perfect-in-beauty-the-enochmetatron-narrative-of-3-enoch-and-ezekiel-28/C4DBC6A40CCFB08153095EF7F74A8A45
4. Catholic Catechism paragraphs 327–336 discuss angels, while paragraphs 66–67 distinguish public Revelation from private revelations: https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_5_heaven_and_earth.html ; https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_one/chapter_two/article_1/iii_christ_jesus_mediator_and_fullness_of_all_revelation.index.html
5. Vatican **Antiqua et nova** (2025) addresses responsible AI, human intelligence, human dignity, relationships, accountability, and the limitations of AI: https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html
