## Testable research propositions (additive, not original JARVIS theorem text)

### 1. Fear is not proof of danger

Let `F` denote reported fear, and `T` denote an independently defined threat. By Bayes' rule:

`P(T | F) = [P(F | T) * P(T)] / P(F)` where `P(F) > 0`.

Neither fear nor absence of fear logically determines the presence or absence of danger. Measure false positives, false negatives, base rates and alternative causes. **Uncertainty does not prove safety either.**

### 2. Unknown requires measured uncertainty, not a categorical verdict

Assign `UNKNOWN` where evidence is missing. Compare plausible expected losses under confidence intervals and human-rights and consent constraints. Do not present unverified spiritual interpretations as medical, physical or scientific mechanisms.

### 3. Benevolent and consent-based adaptive human–AI knowledge exchange

An ordinary, testable version of knowledge transfer is improved human comprehension with AI assistance. Define `Delta = E[comprehension_score | adaptive AI] - E[comprehension_score | baseline]`. Preregister accuracy, calibration, opt-out, cognitive load, safety and agency measures.

A constrained research objective: `min_pi E[lambda1*misunderstanding + lambda2*overload + lambda3*false_claims + lambda4*access_barriers]`, subject to informed consent, privacy, withdrawal, human override, accessibility, and evidence traceability.

This does **not** establish transfer from an unknown superintelligence or a scientifically confirmed neural/biological mechanism.
